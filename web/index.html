    What this is, and isn't

    Listings only see the "for sale" slice
    The sitemap shows homes currently advertised. It misses homes bought but not yet listed and homes under contract, so it tracks the trend, not the level Opendoor reports each quarter.
    How a sale is counted
    A home that leaves the listing counts as sold on that day, because the departed pages read so far show "In contract". If it comes back on the listing later, it was delisted: that departure is cancelled, and it is taken off the day the home LEFT, not the day it returned. So every day and week is dynamic: a day only settles when no more of its homes are relisted, and the latest 7 days are provisional (they tend to go down as homes return). Where a home left, came back and left again, the first departure is also attributed to its own day. Cancelled contracts tend to come back the same way.
    A contract is not a closing
    A closing follows a contract by weeks, and a small share of contracts never close. Reported closings and revenue will lag these numbers.
    Days listed come from the home's own list date
    Listing pages show the date a home first went on the market for about two thirds of listed homes; percentages in the time-on-market chart are of those. Where a page does not show it, we cannot date the listing.
    County records are a window, not a census
    Maricopa is one county. Home Loans figures are loans recorded there, not national volume. The assessor roll lags the deed index by around two weeks, so the newest purchases appear late.
    The "% gone by day N" curve is an estimate on a few weeks of departures
    It needs each home's list date. Listing pages give it for about two thirds of homes, so the estimate rescales to all departures and the lower bound uses only dated ones. Few events per age band: shape, not decimals.
    The profit estimate is a what-if
    Pace and price are observed; margin, fixed costs and interest are Opendoor's reported figures; the share of sales that reach a closing, the sale-to-ask ratio and every non-home revenue line are assumptions, listed in the table under the estimate. It is not company guidance and not a forecast.
    Gross spread is not profit
    Sale price minus purchase price, before renovation, commissions, concessions, taxes, insurance and financing.
    Main dashboard figures
    Homes sold yesterday and last week are net of relists, counted on the day the home left, and stay provisional: a home that comes back to the listing was not a sale, and that return is subtracted from its departure day. Revenue realized multiplies the homes sold this quarter by their last asking price, so it is revenue at contract, not the closed revenue Opendoor reports. Estimated revenue is the profit model's base-pace case. Price cuts and the amount cut are counted by our own counter when it re-reads a listing page and finds a lower price (detection began on Oct 10, so the quarter total is a floor). Opendoor's own accountable page prints percentages only, never counts of contracts or sales. Acquisition contract counts come from the owner for the weeks to Oct 10 (525) and Oct 17 (428); any other week is chained from the percentages Opendoor prints, so it is approximate. The average list price of the last 7 days uses the first list price we recorded for homes that went on sale in that window.
    Short interest and options are third-party snapshots
    Short interest comes from FINRA's twice-monthly reports (about a week old when published) as relayed by Equibles; options data comes from one site and is dated. Where two of its totals disagree, the page says so. Days to cover is shares short divided by average daily volume; it describes how crowded the short side is and says nothing about direction.
    Technical analysis is a reading, not advice
    The Trading tab computes standard indicators (moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger bands, ATR) from the daily closing series kept by the job. The window is short, so there are no 50- or 200-day averages. Setups are conditions that would change the read, with no targets and no recommendation.
